Dua for after the final tash-ahhud… (Hisn al-Muslim 24:64)

Hisn al-Muslim 24

((اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ بِأَنَّ لَكَ الْحَمْدَ لَا إِلَهَ إِلاَّ أَنْتَ وَحْدَكَ لاَ شَرِيكَ لَكَ، الْمَنَّانُ، يَا بَدِيعَ السَّمَوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ يَا ذَا الْجَلاَلِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ، يَا حَيُّ يَا قَيُّومُ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ الْجَنَّةَ وَأَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ النَّارِ)).

(Allahumma innee as-aluka bianna lakal-hamd, la ilaha illa ant wahdaka la shareeka lak, almannan, ya badeeAAas-samawati wal-ard, ya thal-jalali wal-ikram, ya hayyu ya qayyoom, innee as-alukal-jannah, wa-aAAoothu bika minan-nar.)

(O Allah, I ask You as unto You is all praise, none has the right to be worshipped except You, alone, without partner. You are the Benefactor. O Originator of the heavens and the Earth, O Possessor of majesty and honour, O Ever Living, O Self-Subsisting and Supporter of all, verily I ask You for Paradise and I take refuge with You from the Fire.)

Context & meaning

The request is placed last, and everything before it establishes who is being asked. It opens by asking on the strength of praise itself — as unto You is all praise — then states that none has the right to be worshipped except Him, alone and without partner. A run of titles follows, each addressed directly: the Benefactor, Originator of the heavens and the Earth, Possessor of majesty and honour, Ever Living, Self-Subsisting and Supporter of all. Only after that sequence do the two requests arrive, and they point in opposite directions.

When & how to recite

The chapter sets these words in the final sitting, after the Tashahhud and before the prayer ends.

Reflection

Five forms of address stack up before a single thing is requested. What is finally asked for is the shortest part of the supplication: Paradise sought, and refuge taken from the Fire.
