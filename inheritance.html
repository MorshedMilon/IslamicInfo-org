Common Questions

Frequently Asked

What is Faraid (علم الفرائض)? +
The Qur'an dedicates more verses to inheritance than to any other single legal topic. An-Nisa 4:11–12 and 4:176 specify exact fractional shares for each heir category. These are direct divine instruction — not interpretations by scholars or jurists. The word Faraid (الفرائض) means "obligatory shares" — the amounts are fixed by Allah in His Book.
Does this calculator cover all heirs? +
It covers the primary heirs specified in An-Nisa 4:11–12 and 4:176, plus blocking rules (Ḥijb) from Sahih Bukhari 6732 and Muslim 1615. Complex scenarios — multiple grandmothers, great-grandchildren chains, Radd distribution, Waṣiyyah (bequests) — require a qualified scholar, as these involve edge cases not explicitly stated in the Qur'an.
What is Aṣabah (العصبة) — the residue? +
After all Qur'anically specified fixed shares (Fard) are distributed, the remainder goes to the nearest male relative (Aṣabah). The Prophet ﷺ said: "Give the fixed shares to those entitled. What remains goes to the nearest male." (Bukhari 6732, Muslim 1615). This is authenticated Sunnah — not madhab opinion — and is agreed upon by all classical scholars.
Why is there no madhab selector? +
Because the shares this calculator computes are from the Qur'an directly. An-Nisa 4:11 states the shares of children. An-Nisa 4:12 states the shares of spouses. These are not scholarly interpretations — they are Qur'anic text. All four madhabs agree on these shares. Madhab differences apply only to rare edge cases (e.g. the Mushtarakah problem, Radd distribution methods) — none of which are within the scope of this calculator.
My situation is complex — what should I do? +
For situations involving bequests (Waṣiyyah — max 1/3 to non-heirs), non-Muslim relatives, missing heirs, simultaneous deaths, or multiple generations of grandparents — consult a qualified Islamic scholar or a certified Faraid specialist. This calculator handles clear-cut cases directly stated in the Qur'an and authenticated Sunnah.
