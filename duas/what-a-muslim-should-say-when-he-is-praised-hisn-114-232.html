What to say when someone praises you

Hisn al-Muslim 114

((اللَّهُمَّ لاَ تُؤَاخِذْنِي بِمَا يَقُولُونَ، وَاغْفِرْ لِي مَا لاَ يَعْلَمُونَ، [وَاجْعَلْنِي خَيْراً مِمَّا يَظُّنُّونَ])).

Allaahumma laa tu'aakhidhnee bimaa yaqooloona, waghfir lee maa laa ya'lamoona, [waj'alnee khayran mimmaa yazunnoon]

O Allah, do not call me to account for what they say and forgive me for what they have no knowledge of [and make me better than they imagine].

Context & meaning

All three requests concern the gap between what people say and what is true. The first asks not to be held to account for what they say — the praise itself. The second asks forgiveness for what they have no knowledge of, meaning whatever the praise did not see. The third, supplied in brackets by the text, asks to be made better than they imagine. Each clause treats the praise as incomplete rather than deserved.

When & how to recite

The chapter places these words in the moment a Muslim is praised.

Reflection

The praise is never accepted or refused. Every clause measures it against something the praisers do not know, and asks about that instead.

Common questions

Where does this dua come from?

It is recorded at Hisn al-Muslim 114:232.

What do these words mean?

The English of it is: “O Allah, do not call me to account for what they say and forgive me for what they have no knowledge of [and make me better than they imagine].” All three requests concern the gap between what people say and what is true.

Romanised, it runs: “Allaahumma laa tu'aakhidhnee bimaa yaqooloona, waghfir lee maa laa ya'lamoona, [waj'alnee khayran mimmaa yazunnoon]”
