Dua for sleeping (Jami at-Tirmidhi 3485 · 28:102)

Hisn al-Muslim 28

((بِاسْمِكَ رَبِّي وَضَعْتُ جَنْبِي، وَبِكَ أَرْفَعُهُ، فَإِن أَمْسَكْتَ نَفْسِي فارْحَمْهَا، وَإِنْ أَرْسَلْتَهَا فَاحْفَظْهَا، بِمَا تَحْفَظُ بِهِ عِبَادَكَ الصَّالِحِينَ)).

Bismika Rabbee wada'tu janbee, wabika arfa'uh, fa in amsakta nafsee farhamhaa, wa in arsaltahaa fahfazhaa, bimaa tahfazu bihi 'ibaadakas saaliheen

(In Your name my Lord, I lie down and in Your name I rise, so if You should take my soul then have mercy upon it, and if You should return my soul then protect it in the manner You do so with Your righteous servants.)

Context & meaning

The supplication is built on two conditions and does not assume which will follow. Lying down and rising are both placed in His name first. Two outcomes are then set side by side: if the soul is taken, mercy is asked for it; if it is returned, protection is asked for it. Each outcome is given its own request rather than one request stretched to cover both.

When & how to recite

The chapter this belongs to places these words before sleeping.

Reflection

The protection asked for in the second case is defined by comparison — that the soul be protected in the manner His righteous servants are protected — so the request names a standard rather than an amount.

The English of it is: “In Your name my Lord, I lie down and in Your name I rise, so if You should take my soul then have mercy upon it, and if You should return my soul then protect it in the manner You do so with Your righteous servants.” The supplication is built on two conditions and does not assume which will follow.
