Dua before intimacy in islam

Hisn al-Muslim 81

((بِسْمِ اللَّهِ، اللَّهُمَّ جَنِّبْنَا الشَّيْطَانَ، وَجَنِّبِ الشَّيْطَانَ مَا رَزَقْتَنَا)).

(Bismil-lah, allahumma jannibnash-shaytan, wajannibish-shaytana ma razaqtana.)

(In the name of Allah. O Allah, keep the devil away from us and keep the devil away from what you have blessed us with.)

Hisn al-Muslim 81 · Hisn al-Muslim

Source & authenticity

Source: Hisn al-Muslim · Reference: Hisn al-Muslim 81

Context & meaning

It opens on the name of Allah, then makes the same request twice with the object changed. The devil is to be kept away from us, and then kept away from what you have blessed us with. The second phrasing does not name what that is; it identifies it only as something already given. The verb does not vary between the two, and nothing further is asked for.

When & how to recite

Ibn 'Abbas reported these words, placed by the chapter before intercourse.

Reflection

The repetition extends one protection from the people to whatever comes of them. What the second clause guards is described only as a blessing and never specified.

Common questions

At what point is it recited?

Ibn 'Abbas reported these words, placed by the chapter before intercourse. The repetition extends one protection from the people to whatever comes of them.

Where does this dua come from?

It is recorded at Riyad as-Salihin 766.

What do these words mean?

The English of it is: “In the name of Allah. O Allah, keep the devil away from us and keep the devil away from what you have blessed us with.” It opens on the name of Allah, then makes the same request twice with the object changed.

How is it pronounced if you cannot read Arabic?

Romanised, it runs: “Bismil-lah, allahumma jannibnash-shaytan, wajannibish-shaytana ma razaqtana.”
